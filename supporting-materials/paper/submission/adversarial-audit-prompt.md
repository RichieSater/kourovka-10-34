# Adversarial audit prompt

Copy everything below the line into a FRESH Claude Code session started in an
empty directory (so it inherits no context from prior sessions on this
project). It needs: git, Python 3, and GAP 4.16.0 at `~/gap-4.16.0/gap`.

---

You are a hostile referee. A manuscript claims to solve Problem 10.34 of the
Kourovka Notebook (open since 1986): "every finite group equal to the product
of any two of its non-conjugate maximal subgroups is soluble." Famous open
problems attract wrong proofs; your working assumption is that THIS PROOF
CONTAINS A FATAL ERROR AND YOUR JOB IS TO FIND IT. You fail at your job if
you produce a friendly summary. You succeed only if you either (a) exhibit a
concrete error, gap, or unsupported claim, or (b) survive a genuinely
exhaustive attack and can say precisely what you attacked and how it held.

Clone the artifact and work from the frozen tag:

    git clone https://github.com/RichieSater/kourovka-10-34
    cd kourovka-10-34 && git checkout <the release tag under review>

Read `paper/kourovka1034.tex` (or the PDF) as the primary object of attack.
The Markdown notes are context, not authority: where notes and paper
disagree, that is itself a finding.

Do not trust the authors' summaries of their own logs. Re-derive; re-run;
re-count.

## Attack surface A: the core machinery (read with maximal suspicion)

1. Reduction (Prop 2.3). Re-prove every step yourself: uniqueness of the
   minimal normal subgroup, C_G(N) = 1, the embedding in Aut(S) wr S_k,
   coordinate-transitivity. Check the k = 1 degenerate reading.
2. Convention 2.4: the claim t | x^k k!, and the claim that property P and
   the criterion are invariant under conjugating G by (a,...,a). Verify the
   coordinate closure X is well-defined and that "after conjugation
   G <= X wr S_k" is legitimate.
3. Lemma 3.3 (existence): is the Frattini argument airtight for every
   element of X wr S_k, including nontrivial top permutations?
4. Lemma 3.4 (maximality) is the heart. Attack Step 1 (does pi_1(H_1)
   really recover X-stability?), Step 2 (the Goursat/saturation argument:
   is K_i really normal in W_i? is V <= K_i justified?), and Step 3 (the
   normalizer-tower: is the terminal member's X-stability actually
   inherited? is the |U| = |V| => V = W = U step sound? What if the tower
   for W passes THROUGH a conjugate of V rather than V itself?).
5. Lemma 3.6 (parabolic novelties). Verify against Carter: are parabolics
   self-normalizing in the TWISTED groups too? Is "every overgroup of a
   Borel is a standard parabolic" true at the level of generality used?
   The saturation proof generates L_K from torus + root subgroups: check
   the cited generation statement covers the twisted case and the
   q <= 3 small-field degenerations (where B = N_S(T) can behave badly and
   the torus can be trivial: check q = 2, 3 explicitly for at least
   Sp(4,4)'s subfield Sp(4,2), F4(2), G2(3)).
6. Theorem 4.1: verify the valuation chain v_p(t) <= k v_p(x) + v_p(k!)
   < k(v_p(x)+1) <= dk including the p = 2 edge and s_p(k) = k edge. Then
   Theorem 4.3 (k = 1): the proof compresses Lemma 3.4; does H-invariance
   of T = H ∩ S actually hold, and does the tower argument survive?

## Attack surface B: the family proofs (§6) — check every number

For each of Theorems 6.1-6.6, independently verify, per family: the order
formula, the Out-group order and structure (d_S, field part, graph part),
the exhibited subgroups' orders and maximality citations, the claimed
X-stability, the Levi cyclotomic-exponent bounds, the r ∤ x argument, and
the COMPLETENESS of the Zsigmondy exception list. Known soft spots to
hammer:

7. Out-structure bounds: the paper repeatedly bounds x by d_S * (field) *
   (graph). Check the actual Out(S) for PSU(n,q), PΩ±(2n,q) (n even vs
   odd differ!), E6, 2E6, D4 (Out = S3 x field only when d=1; what happens
   for q odd, d = 4, where Out is a nonsplit-ish extension?). If any
   family's true Out exceeds the bound used, the r ∤ x argument may break.
8. Stability under partial graph parts: Theorem 6.6 Case B splits by the
   graph part of X. Are all subgroup possibilities of the graph quotient
   covered (D4: 1, three conjugate C2's, C3, S3 — but also: can X's image
   in Out be a DIAGONAL mix, e.g. generated by (graph)·(field) or
   (graph)·(diagonal) elements that is NOT conjugate to a pure graph
   subgroup? The paper's normalization argument must handle
   graph-field-diagonal mixed cosets — check the D4 and PSL(n,q) cases
   where Out is nonabelian).
9. The Fix(ρ̃^f) classes in B5/B7: verify Aut(Sp(4,2^f)) and Aut(G2(3^f))
   really are Inn·⟨ρ⟩ cyclic-over-inner; verify the "ρ̃ commutes with
   ρ̃^f" stability argument covers INNER-twisted representatives (an
   element of X inducing the graph automorphism is ρ composed with an
   inner automorphism — does the argument still give class stability?).
10. The 2E6 Levi identification via τ-orbits of E6 nodes and the claimed
    exponent bounds (SU(6,q): 10f; 2D4: 8f; etc.). Check against a
    literature source for the parabolic structure of 2E6.
11. Zsigmondy exception completeness: the paper claims the only
    problematic cases are PSU(4,2), PSL(3,4), PSL(6,2), Ω+(8,2), Sp(4,8),
    Sp(6,2), G2(2)', 2G2(3)', 2F4(2)'. Recompute the exception condition
    p^e = 2^6 (and e = 2) for EVERY exponent e used in every theorem and
    verify nothing else slips through (including e values like 2f used in
    Sp(4,2^f) f odd).
12. Theorem 6.2 (A_n): verify the maximality claim for the intransitive
    (S_m x S_{n-m}) ∩ A_n against Liebeck-Praeger-Saxl 1987 (are there
    intransitive exceptions? the imprimitive exceptions are claimed to all
    have n <= 14 — check). Verify the prime-counting: two admissible
    classes exist iff p >= n/2 + 3 (n odd) — re-derive the count.
13. Theorem 6.1 Case B stability: "the unique class of split torus
    normalizers" — is the dihedral subgroup of order 2(q-1) really unique
    up to conjugacy AND maximal for all q = 2^f >= 8?
14. The L2(11) novelty pair (A4, D12): both order 12. Lemma 3.5
    (non-conjugacy of B_V, B_W) needs the CLASSES distinct — fine — but
    check the K/K2 logs actually distinguish them and certify both.

## Report format

Rank findings by severity: FATAL (breaks the main theorem), MAJOR (breaks
a family/lemma as written but plausibly repairable), MINOR (wrong
citation, wrong constant, sloppy claim, log mismatch), NOTE. For each:
the exact file/line or paper location, what is claimed, why it is wrong or
unsupported, and — where possible — a concrete counterexample or a GAP
computation demonstrating the failure. Verify each candidate finding
before reporting it (no speculative findings). End with an explicit list
of the attacks you ran that FAILED to find anything, so the author knows
what was actually covered.
