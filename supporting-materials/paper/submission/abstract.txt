We prove that every finite group that is the product of every pair of
its non-conjugate maximal subgroups is soluble, answering Problem
10.34 of the Kourovka Notebook (V. S. Monakhov, 1986) in the negative.
Tikhonenko and Tyutyanov settled the almost simple case in 2010. What remains
is the possibility of a minimal counterexample whose unique minimal normal
subgroup is S^k, with S non-abelian simple and k >= 2. Because k is unbounded,
no bound depending on a fixed ratio of subgroup orders can handle all such
groups. We rule out this possibility with a divisibility criterion: a single
pair of automorphism-stable conjugacy classes of subgroups of S, satisfying a
valuation inequality, excludes the configuration for every k >= 2 and every
admissible embedding. Such pairs are constructed uniformly for every infinite
family of finite simple groups, with Zsigmondy primes as
the arithmetic obstruction. The sporadic groups and the finitely many
exceptional cases are handled by independently checkable GAP certificates.
